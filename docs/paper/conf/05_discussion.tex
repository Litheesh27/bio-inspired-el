\section{Discussion}

\textbf{Why a real road network was necessary.} The maximum-flux walk fails
by \emph{dead-ending} --- reaching a node with no admissible unvisited
successor. On a complete graph that is impossible, and on a small-world of
mean degree 6--600 it is vanishingly rare. Our network has mean out-degree
2.46, and the undirected pressure solve routinely prefers a direction the
road forbids. The failure mode we measure is structurally unreachable on the
substrates where this model's accuracy was established. That reframes the
disagreement with prior work as a matter of domain rather than correctness:
Zhang et al.\ need no extraction machinery at all because on
Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi graphs the flux separates
cleanly~\cite{zhang2014improved}, and Wang et al.\ are describing undirected
graphs where the walk cannot dead-end~\cite{wang2021transition}. Neither is
wrong. What changes on a directed, sparse, organically grown network is that
extraction stops being a formality and becomes the step that decides the
reported outcome.

\textbf{Provenance as a reporting requirement.} A Physarum accuracy figure
on such a network is a statement about the extraction policy at least as
much as about the model, and a pooled number is unfalsifiable --- our 90\%
and our 23.3\% come from the same solves. Any evaluation of this model class
on a directed network should publish the provenance distribution of its
reported paths, and a model whose fallback is a shortest-path algorithm
should report what share of its results that fallback produced. The stakes
are concrete: a stranded-component degeneracy we found and fixed caused
non-finite pressures under which every flux and threshold test silently
failed, producing a fabricated mean stretch of exactly 1.000 on the
scenario where the model is weakest, with no error raised. Provenance
instrumentation is what made it visible.

\textbf{What self-healing is worth here.} The adaptive state is real and
substantial --- single-iteration recovery on nearly half of all closures, at
unchanged per-iteration cost, which is the strongest form of the claim we can
support. But it is paid for in path quality, on both the reported path and
the model's own extraction. A system that must guarantee route quality after
a closure should prefer a cold solve or a recompute; one that must respond
within a single adaptation step and can tolerate a few percent of path cost
is where retained state pays. Neither conclusion follows from the speed
measurement alone. And the baseline stands: Dijkstra recomputes an exact
route in about 31\,ms here, so on a network of this size recomputation is
simply the right engineering answer, and the case for adaptive methods must
rest on regimes we did not test --- far larger networks, many simultaneous
commodities, or settings where the cost function is not globally observable.

\textbf{Limitations.} One city and one bounding box; the structural
properties we argue are decisive are common to dense organically grown
cities but transfer is not demonstrated. Congestion comes from synthetic
demand rather than calibrated counts, which is where the closest recent ACO
work is stronger~\cite{tuan2026improved}; we traded measured demand for a
controlled, repeatable closure protocol over a full directed street network.
Absolute times are optimistic because the shared baseline re-annotates
free-flow speeds, so we report only ratios. Physarum is run per OD pair
rather than as multi-commodity flow, and we did not implement the directed
formulation~\cite{zhang2014directed}, so our extraction findings
characterise the canonical model on a directed network --- an acknowledged
limitation of that model --- rather than the limits of Physarum-style
methods in principle. ACO was tuned while Physarum was near-default, a real
asymmetry, though the extraction findings concern a structural failure mode
rather than a parameter setting.

\section{Conclusion}

Benchmarking classical, ant-colony and Physarum routing on a real directed
road network under microsimulated congestion, over 5{,}520 paired runs, we
find that the self-healing claim holds in its strongest form --- warm start
recovers in a single iteration on 48.3\% of closures, which cold start never
achieves, at unchanged per-iteration cost --- but that it is a trade, being
significantly worse on the reported post-disruption path. The finding we
think matters most is methodological: a Physarum model emits a field, not a
route, and on a sparse directed network the extraction step decides the
answer. Pooled, our Physarum matches the optimum on 90\% of pairs; using
only its own walk, 23.3\%; on warm recoveries it returns Dijkstra's path
two-thirds of the time. Because the median stretch is exactly 1.000 inside
every provenance class, a pooled figure cannot be falsified, and we argue
provenance should be reported as a matter of course. Throughout, Dijkstra
remained the honest baseline that no method here approached on wall-clock.
